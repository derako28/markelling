### Вариант 1
Johnny Depp earned $650 million from movies that grossed $3.6 billion. By 2018, almost all of it was gone.

For 17 years, his money ran through The Management Group, a firm owned by brothers Joel and Robert Mandel.

In January 2017 Depp sued them for $25 million. His claims: they took 5% of his gross - more than $28 million - with no written contract. They filed his taxes late, which cost him $5.7 million in penalties and interest. They lent nearly $10 million of his money to other people without asking him.

Two weeks later the Mandels sued back. Their number: more than $2 million a month. 14 homes. A 150-foot yacht. $3 million to fire Hunter S. Thompson's ashes out of a cannon. $30,000 a month on wine.

"Depp, and Depp alone, is fully responsible," their filing said.

In 2018 Rolling Stone asked him about the wine.

"It's insulting to say that I spent $30,000 on wine," he said. "Because it was far more."

The cannon, he added, was $5 million, not $3 million.

The case for victim: he paid people 5% of everything to watch the money, and the taxes still came in late.

The case for careless millionaire: he knew the managers' totals were wrong. He thought they were too low.

The suit settled in July 2018. The terms were never made public.

Here's the lesson. A handshake fee is an assumption you renew every month without reading it.

In the video, four years later, a lawyer in a Virginia courtroom asks him if he poured himself "a mega pint" of red wine. Depp repeats "A mega pint?" and the room laughs. Then: "I poured myself a large glass of wine. I thought it necessary."

Watch him, then decide.

Victim or careless millionaire?

### Вариант 2
Johnny Depp's former managers told a court he spent $30,000 a month on wine.

Depp's answer, to Rolling Stone in 2018: "It's insulting to say that I spent $30,000 on wine. Because it was far more."

He wasn't denying the habit. He was correcting the math.

That is the whole Depp - Mandel fight in one line. Two sides, two sets of numbers, and nobody holding the total.

Depp's numbers, January 2017, in a $25 million lawsuit: the Mandel brothers' firm, The Management Group, took 5% of his gross for years - more than $28 million - on a handshake. Late tax filings cost him $5.7 million. Nearly $10 million of his money went out as loans he says he never approved.

The Mandels' numbers, two weeks later: more than $2 million a month. 14 homes. A 150-foot yacht. $3 million to shoot Hunter S. Thompson's ashes into the sky.

Depp corrected that one too. "It was $5 million."

He had earned about $650 million from his films. Rolling Stone reported almost all of it was gone.

The case for the managers: they say they warned him again and again, and he kept spending.

The case for Depp: they were paid 5% of everything to keep the books, and the IRS was still sending penalties.

It settled in July 2018. Nobody disclosed the terms.

Here's the lesson. A bill nobody adds up always looks smaller than it is.

In the video, it's 2022, a courtroom in Virginia. A lawyer asks if he poured himself "a mega pint" of red wine. Depp repeats "A mega pint?" and the room laughs. Then: "I poured myself a large glass of wine. I thought it necessary."

Watch him, then decide.

Robbed by his managers, or by his own wine list?
